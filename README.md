# Institutions from interaction

How a division of labour, roles held in mutual expectations, emerges among independent learning agents that
perceive one another only through signs they learn to display and signs they learn to seek.

The paper is under double-blind review, so the documents here are the anonymous versions and carry no author
information. They will be replaced by the version of record when the review is decided.

- `paper/paper_anonymous.pdf` — the paper, 9 pages.
- `paper/supplement_anonymous.pdf` — the supplement, 58 pages: the model in full, the exact definition of every
  measurement, the predictions registered before the runs and their outcomes, every set with its per-seed
  numbers, and a table for every world at every reading.
- `companion/` — the engine, the world generators, the readers, and every reading file behind every number.
- `CORRECTIONS.md` — the two places where these documents differ from the version submitted for review.

What the work shows, in one paragraph. Sixteen learning agents and eleven consumers trade programs in a market
where an agent perceives another only through a sign the other has learned to display, and chooses whom to buy
from only through a sign it has learned to seek. Every run is paired with a sign-blind twin on the same seed, in
which the signs are learned and paid for but no longer steer the choice. With the signs, sellers sorted into
roles that an outsider could tell apart from the signs alone, buyers obtained more of what they wanted, and
where no agent could build a whole product within its own capacity every whole that consumers paid for was
assembled from parts bought from other agents. The roles and the assembling teams outlived the agents that
formed them under a turnover that replaced nearly every founder.
